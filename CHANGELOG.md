The seven flattest patterns got stronger contrast (measured factor std over a 41x41 grid: weathered wood 0.016 up to 0.042, limestone 0.028 to 0.050, raw concrete 0.028 to 0.043, wool loop 0.031 to 0.047, galvanised 0.035 to 0.048, burl 0.035 to 0.048, slate 0.037 to 0.048), all inside the pinned factor range; the three legacy patterns keep their golden defaults. Pinned by QtFColorTests (group C27): the promised colors per style, the look resolution and the explicit-color-wins rule, the segment defaults and the saved-values rule, the Z stub geometry, the pattern contrast floor, the one-undo-step pick, the hover preview, and a pixel check that a wood box renders brown and a brick cylinder renders red-brown. 614 tests passing.

- F-FIRE — The Fire emitter no longer ghosts the viewport: adding an emitter with the Fire preset (the default) used to wash out the whole frame — mean brightness 50 up to 68, blown-white pixels, floor see-through, grid showing through — and it stayed broken after the emitter was deleted. Root-caused with a GL-state trace (per-pass glGet probes at every pass boundary), not a guess: Qt hands paintGL a context with blending ENABLED (its own compositing uses it and never turns it off), and the particle pass set the blend func to additive (SRC_ALPHA, ONE) for fire and sparks and restored the enable and the depth mask but never the func. With the default func (ONE, ZERO) the enabled blending was invisible, so the clean scene looked right; after one additive frame the leaked func made every later opaque pass add instead of replace, and deleting the emitter changed nothing because the func is sticky GL state and the empty particle pass returns before touching it. Rain never triggered it because its leaked func (SRC_ALPHA, ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA) is invisible on opaque fragments. The fix is two layers: `_paint_scene` re-asserts the base GL state at the start of every frame (blend off, blend func back to normal alpha, depth mask on, depth test on), so no frame depends on what Qt or a previous frame left; and `_draw_particles` restores the blend func with the enable and the depth mask, so the pass leaves nothing sticky behind. Measured on the tavern at 800x600: Fire live 50.1 mean brightness (was 68.4), emitter deleted 49.9 (was 68.5), blown pixels 0.0000 throughout. Pinned by QtFireGhostRegressionTests (group C26), which check the WHOLE frame (mean brightness + blown-white pixels, the audit's method) with a Fire emitter live and after deleting it, plus the same check for Sparks — the old T17 checks only looked at particle pixels, which is why this was never caught. The new tests fail on the unfixed code (68.4 mean) and pass on the fixed code. 600 tests passing.

- PO5 — Weather and age: materials now weather. Every style takes the wear its material would — dirt, grime, edge wear, moss, and wetness on wood, stone, brick, concrete, and ground; rust on metals instead of moss; cracks on stone, brick, and concrete only; fabric takes dirt, grime, and wetness — and a style-less material takes the generic set, so wear overlays any material, even a plain colour. The wear is a shader-side overlay in the PBR fragment: procedural noise masks, placed where they gather as far as the shader can tell — dirt settles on up-facing surfaces and darkens toward brown, grime gathers in shaded low spots, edge wear lightens exposed faces, rust tints orange-brown in clustered patches and roughens, moss tints green on damp up-facing patches, wetness darkens and smooths (roughness drops where wet), and cracks are a Voronoi edge field at the stored spacing in centimetres, polygonal fissures the way stone and concrete actually crack. Honest scope: true edge/cavity placement needs curvature data Reblend does not carry, so the v1 masks are noise + height/angle based — they read as weathering, not as measured wear. Wear at 0% costs nothing and changes no pixel: the overlay is gated on the wear uniforms, and when every value is 0 the block never runs (pinned by a byte-identical render test). The material dict gains `wear` (overrides only like `params` — an empty dict means clean and dry, no cracks; cracks store the fissure spacing in cm, 0 meaning off), and old scenes load clean. The Life rows live in the Material section, Advanced-only, after the layout rows; a wear drag is one undo step per gesture (a gesture ending at 0 stores nothing), picking a style clears the wear in the pick's own step, and hovering a style previews it clean with the stored wear surviving the hover. Pinned by MaterialWearTests (headless, in A2) + QtMaterialWearTests (group C24). 583 tests passing.

- PO4 — Layouts + real-world sizes: materials now lay out in real centimetres. Wood takes Solid, Boards, Parquet, Chevron, Herringbone, and Strip (burl takes Solid, Boards, Strip); stone and concrete take Solid and Slabs (slate also Strip); brick takes Stretcher, Flemish, English, Basket Weave, and Herringbone bonds; metals, fabrics, and grounds take Solid only and hide the picker. The layout is a shader-side pattern transform: the fragment shader samples the pattern from the object's own surface in local centimetres (a new `vLocalPos` varying, planar projection per dominant face axis), so a 12 cm board is 12 cm on any object — no geometry change, no UVs. Boards stagger their joints, parquet and basket weave alternate block orientations, herringbone and chevron share one staircase lattice (proven to tile exactly by a dense-grid coverage check), slabs grid plainly, and every piece shows different grain via a per-piece hash offset with a 0.5 cm darkened joint. Brick bonds are the exception: they rearrange the bricks, which only the bake can do, so they are baked into the tile (the stretcher path is bit-identical to before) and the shader only tiles them at real-world size. The material dict gains `layout` (resolved against the style, so a stored layout from another style becomes the style's default — brick defaults to Stretcher, everything else to Solid) and `layout_size` (board width + length in cm, overrides only like `params`; the picker relabels the same two keys per layout, so sizes persist across layout switches, and brick sizes itself from the brick length / course height knobs instead). The picker and the size rows live in the Material section, Advanced-only, after the param knobs; picking a style resets the layout to the new style's default in the same undo step, a layout pick is one undo step, a size drag is one step per gesture (a gesture ending on the default stores nothing), and hovering a layout previews it with the UI12 semantics. Solid renders exactly as before (the vUV path is untouched when the mode is 0), so the tavern shots stay byte-identical. Honest limits: sizes are exact on unscaled objects (the transform reads the pre-scale local position, so an object scale stretches the pattern); on curved surfaces the pattern shows seams where the projection axis changes (grain-follows-shape is out of scope); the brick bonds tile seamlessly on the default 8-row / 4-column grid and show a bond irregularity, never a gap, on other grids. Pinned by MaterialLayoutsTests (headless, in A2) + QtMaterialLayoutTests (group C23). 563 tests passing.

- T18 — Stepped particles: a second particle mode alongside the analytic closed form. The mode is automatic from the parameters: an emitter runs stepped when its floor behavior is bounce, or when it attracts toward another object with a nonzero strength; everything else stays analytic (turbulence alone does not switch modes — in analytic mode it is the sine wobble, in stepped mode it is the curl strength). Stepped mode is a fixed-timestep deterministic re-simulation from frame 0 with per-frame memoization (semi-implicit Euler, one step per frame, births get a partial step from their exact birth time), so scrubbing forward, backward, and repeated visits all return bit-identical particle states. Turbulence is true curl noise: the curl of a 3-field hash-based value-noise potential (stdlib only, Perlin permutation lattice, analytic gradients, no finite differences), divergence-free by construction, so smoke drifts and swirls with the wind. Floor bounce reflects with restitution 0.4; a bounce whose apex would be under 1 cm settles into a rest on the floor instead of micro-bouncing (an impact-speed threshold cannot work — one step of gravity alone is 41 cm/s), and a resting particle stays at rest until an upward force lifts it. Attraction is a spring pull toward the target object (strength in 1/s^2, negative repels), so sparks orbit their target; a deleted target leaves the pull off without crashing, and floor kill still kills in stepped mode. The per-emitter cap (2,000, oldest die first) is applied to the stepped state, and the capped/dropped report comes from a death-time heap tracking the uncapped live count — the state alone cannot say how many live particles the cap hid. The emitter editor's attract and bounce help lines now say they work (they said "arrives with stepped particles" before). The analytic sim is unchanged in behavior (proven bit-identical on 70 golden cases across every preset, windy worlds, an animated emitter, and the heavy 2,000-live case) but restructured for speed: the T17 follow-up is resolved — sustained 2,000 live now costs 13-23 ms/frame warm in the sandbox (was 60-161 ms), under the 41.7 ms budget for 24 fps, from hoisted per-particle parameters, a drag-free/gust-free fast path, a static-emitter path that skips the animation sampler, and a merge that builds plain lists and converts once. Stepped 2,000 live with curl costs about 33 ms/frame incremental (under budget); a cold jump to frame 48 with curl re-simulates all 48 frames and takes about 2.4 s in the sandbox — the design-accepted scrub cost at max load, milliseconds at typical loads. Pinned by SteppedParticleTests (headless, in A2) + QtSteppedParticleTests (group C17). 472 tests passing.

- UI10 + UI11 — Live measurements + self-tuning viewport quality: the info row under the viewport shows the measured frame rate next to the object and triangle counts — frames per second and milliseconds per frame, a rolling average over the last 30 paints, display only. The measurement is paint cost (a timestamp around paintGL), not the gap between paints: the gap is dominated by the playback timer and by idle time between demand paints, while the cost moves with scene load, which is what the readout is for. Nothing shows until 10 paints are measured, so a single-frame grab shows the same empty row every run (the regression shots stay byte-identical); the first paint after a scene load is skipped from the window, since it builds every VBO at once and is a setup cost, not the sustained rate. Behind the readout, the viewport tunes its own cost silently (UI11): it steps down a quality ladder when frames run slow and back up when they are fast again — level 0 full quality (1024 shadow map, full render scale), level 1 smaller shadow map (512), level 2 shadows off, level 3 render scale 0.75, level 4 render scale 0.5 — with no user-visible setting anywhere. The steps are hysteresis-guarded so it never flickers: 30 consecutive slow samples (rolling average over the 41.7 ms budget, the 24 fps floor) to step down, 60 consecutive fast samples (average under 20 ms) to step back up, a dead band between the two thresholds where the level holds, and a 30-paint settle after each level change while the rolling window flushes the old level's costs. Only sustained painting counts — a paint is a load sample only when the app idled under 500 ms before it (measured end of the previous paint to start of this one, so a slow paint does not count against itself), so single still frames (and the regression shots) never engage the tuner. Render scale renders the scene into a smaller framebuffer and upscales it into the widget with a fullscreen triangle; the shadow map rebuilds lazily on the next paint, where the GL context is current. Exported frames and video always render at full quality: the export paths hold a re-entrant full-quality override (per grab and around the whole export loop), so a stepped-down viewport never silently degrades an export. Pinned by QtFrameStatsTests (group C15) + QtViewportTunerTests (group C16). No visible change to the default window: the readout is hidden until 10 paints are measured, and the regression shots paint fewer than that before the grab. 457 tests passing.
